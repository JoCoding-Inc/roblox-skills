# Reveal cutscene builds: what the judges named, build by build

The record behind the rules of `roblox-feel-audio-vfx` section 3e: a reveal cutscene (a rare roll, 10-15 s) built again and again by one smaller model from the served guide, each build judged blind against a VFX artist's posted cut (two judges per sitting, sums out of 100 unless stated), 2026-11-21 to 2026-11-22. Each table names what the judges marked down, the rule taken from it and what changed in the modules. Quotes are the judges' own words. Section 3e holds the rules as they stand now; where a later build changed a number, the later one wins there. "Above" and "below" in the record point within it; the modules it names are described in section 3e.

**A reveal cutscene (a rare roll, an unlock, 10-15 s).** The anatomy above is a 7 s strike. A reveal runs longer and travels, and a build made from this section alone lost a blind comparison with a VFX artist's posted 15 s roll reveal, 50 against 69 (sums of two judges out of 100, 2026-11-21; one comparison; a second build made with the rules below scored 63 against 76). It was level on structure and ahead on its counter and tier colours; it lost on the shapes of its effects (4 against 8 of 10), on its last shot and on its sky. The artist's cut, read at 30 fps:

| Shot | Time s | What |
|---|---|---|
| Dark start | 0-1.2 | black screen, one thin four-point star that grows, turns and changes hue (green, blue, violet); the odds counter already runs |
| Whip out | 1.2-2.1 | a cut to an extreme close-up of the face behind the star, then a fast pull-back into a wide sunset sky, the character spread out and small props drifting round it |
| Drift | 2.1-3.2 | two wide shots from above and beside, the character small, the camera turning |
| Energy arrives | 3.2-5.3 | ribbons with a star at the head come in one, then three, then four or five run parallel behind the flying character in a side shot, then wind round the body |
| First burst | 5.3-6.4 | crystal stars open beside the character, one white four-point flash that fills a third of the frame for 2 frames |
| Hover | 6.4-7.4 | a calm wide shot, the character glowing, small arcs round it |
| Bloom | 7.4-8.7 | crystal bursts open outward on turning ribbon arms, each bigger than the last |
| Peak | 8.8-10.7 | a full white frame, then a held close-up (1.6 s): the face ringed by crystal stars over a radial speed-line background, a slow push-in |
| Exit | 10.9-13.4 | a cut to a streak that shoots to the horizon, then a golden cross-star with thin rays and two rings over the sky; the counter lands on its last number about 1 s into this shot |
| Out | 13.6-14.2 | white-out, then a fade through grey |

Rules both judges gave for the weaker build (judgment from one comparison; the numbers are theirs):

- **Designed shapes, not soft dots.** Thin four-point stars (about 8 long to 1 wide), hard-pointed crystal or shard bursts, ribbons that taper to nothing with a star at the head. Soft round glows are under a fifth of what is on screen. A flat beam drawn as one quad and plain grey speed lines read as unfinished: taper the trail (a white core, the tier colour falling off) and tint speed lines in the tier colour.
- **The effects belong to the character.** They arrive at it, run behind it, wind round it and open from it; at the reveal the reward is on the character (ribbons that orbit at 1.5-2 studs, an aura, a shape that fits the name), not only in a title.
- **The character is never small for long and never in its default pose.** In a travel shot it fills 15-35% of the frame height; a shot where it is under a tenth lasts under a second. Every held shot has an authored pose (an overshoot and settle of about 0.3 s, then a small idle) and a moving camera (a 5-8% push-in or a 10-15 degree orbit from a low three-quarter angle). A T-pose on a locked camera was the first fix both judges asked for.
- **Cut from close to wide.** The strongest cut in the artist's piece is an extreme close-up that whips out to the widest shot. Follow it with different angles (high, top-down, side), and keep a static opening wide under about 0.6 s or give it a push-in.
- **The counter never passes its last number.** Ease it out over the last 1.5 s so only the last digits tick, and land it on the peak's flash frame or in the first second of the last shot, with a scale punch (1 -> 1.3 -> 1 in 0.25 s). A counter that overshot to 7.2 million and came back to 2.4 million was named by both judges.
- **A flash is white and short.** 2-3 frames of full white (or the tier colour near white), then a 0.15 s fall. A grey washed frame reads as an error; so does a near-black frame with no rim light on the character.
- **A sky to fly through.** Fog in one flat colour read as beige haze. Use a sky with cloud shapes that the camera can pass: a `Clouds` object under `Terrain` (not measured by us: start near `Cover` 0.6 and `Density` 0.7 with a warm `Color`), a low sun (`ClockTime` near 6.3 or 17.7) and an `Atmosphere` whose haze does not hide the horizon. Keep full-screen blur and vignette light in wide shots.
- **The title clears the head.** Put the name in the top fifth of the frame or frame the character lower; bring it in within 0.3 s (scale 1.3 -> 1).

Sharp shapes without uploads (`fx_recipe`, 3i; Studio Play captures on a dark wall, 2026-11-21; not judged blind yet): the kind `spikes` draws pointed rays as tapered beams, so a star is a drawn shape at any size, and four recipes use it: `star_glint` (one four-point star that opens, turns and closes: the star in a dark start, a sparkle on a reward), `shard_burst` (12 pointed shards thrown out of a star with one ring: a beat of the build, a crystal breaking) and `reveal_star` (a 14-stud cross star with a second star turned 45 degrees, two rings and a held light: the exit shot). `crown` is the motif to repeat: crystals standing in a ring round the anchor, points out, dealt one after another from the top and turning slowly, with a thin ring through their tails and a held light. `FxRecipe.crown(count)` returns it with that many crystals (more crystals stand a little further out and longer; from 8 up every second one is short), so one element grows through the scene: `FxRecipe.play(FxRecipe.crown(3), character.Head, { depth = -2, palette = "gold" })` at the first beat, `crown(6)` at the middle, `crown(12)` with `duration = 3` behind the title. The ring and the crystals lie flat to the camera, so start it after the cut to the shot that shows it (seen on slow-motion stills in Studio Play at 3, 6 and 12 crystals in four palettes, 2026-11-21; not recorded at real speed and not judged blind yet). `trail_star` is a ribbon that tapers to nothing with a star turning at its head; play it on an invisible part that you move (`FxRecipe.play("trail_star", part, { follow = true, duration = 3 })`), one part per ribbon, and send the parts past, behind and round the character. Change the colour with `palette`. Do not use the `star` slot of `fx_textures` for a designed star: its first texture is a ragged spark, and the built-in sparkle has eight thin rays (a builder's finding, 2026-11-21).

What a second build still lost on after using the rules above (63 against 76, sums of two judges; the first build scored 47 in the same sitting; judgment from that one comparison, numbers theirs):

- **No untextured particles.** Flat pink rectangles and squares round the character read as default sprites or bare parts. Every particle has a shape: a star, a shard, a glow, a streak.
- **Haze at half.** One fog colour over ten seconds read as muddy; the character at 5-8% of the frame height inside it was lost. Halve the `Atmosphere` density, keep the sun in frame for contrast, and frame the character at 25-35% in the sky shots with a slow orbit or push-in.
- **A burst keeps its shape.** A bloom that turned a third of the frame into a white cloud erased the character and the rays. During a burst lower `BloomEffect.Intensity` by about 40% and raise its `Threshold`; make the flash itself a four-point star for 2-3 frames (`star_glint` at a large `scale`) instead of a flat white frame.
- **The character is in every shot.** A beat of empty sky with one streak, and an unmotivated profile shot before the title, broke the build. Cut from the close-up straight to the title shot and spend the time on a longer hold.
- **The last frame is composed.** The face at about half the frame height, the whole upper body above the counter's label, the star behind and above the head, not on it. Hold the title about 2 s with a slow 10-15 degree orbit, then fade from it over 0.5 s (the twelfth build, below).
- **Leave through a fade.** A white frame that cuts to the bare game world read as unfinished. Dip to black over 0.5 s with the name and the counter still on, and give the camera back under it. The letterbox, the name, the counter and the graded sky stay until the black is full (`ScreenOverlays.fade` holds them for you). Give the hold a last beat before the fade: a 0.5 s pull-back to a wide, or one more ring off the motif, in the title's last half second. A hold that only dims was named "no ending beat" (one judge, 2026-11-21; judgment, not built into a module).
- **Depth in the sky.** Two or three layers pass the camera at different speeds: near cloud wisps, small floating props, far clouds. One flat backdrop has no parallax.
- **One motif that grows.** Pick one element and bring it back larger at each beat (`FxRecipe.crown(3)`, then 6, then 12 behind the title) in place of a different loose burst every time. Two judges in two sittings named the missing signature; the fill around it (dust, glints) stays small and soft.
- **A mark at the middle.** At a round number on the counter, a ring of 8-12 large shards out of the character and a change of sky colour for about a second (`shard_burst`), so the build has a step before the peak.

Found by that builder, not measured by us: a black void for a dark start is `Atmosphere` `Density` 1 and `Offset` 1 with black colours, `ClockTime` 0 and `Lighting.Brightness` 0 (blank sky textures still drew the default sky); a `BillboardGui` drawn about 1,900 px wide did not render while 100 px ones did, so large flat shapes were drawn in a `ScreenGui` placed with `WorldToViewportPoint` (or as `spikes`, which are beams); a `CanvasGroup` did not draw inside a `BillboardGui`.

**Modules for a cutscene above the map** (added 2026-11-21 after a third build; Studio 0.748 Play captures with an avatar on the stage; not judged blind yet). A third build made with every rule above scored 58 against 68 for the artist's cut and 61 for the second build (sums of two judges, three versions in one sitting). Both judges gave it the same list: a flat pink haze with no horizon, a default T-pose in two shots, soft white spheres beside the character, a title that came two seconds before the counter landed, one black frame and then the title over the bare map at the end. Each of those was something the builder had to invent from a rule, so each is a module now:

| Need | Module | Use |
|---|---|---|
| A sky with shapes | `cine_sky.luau` | `local sky = Sky.enter("sunset", { focus = root.Position })` lifts the stage 1,200 studs over the map (`sky.origin`); `sky:stage(character)` anchors the root, moves the character there and hides its ForceField, and `sky:leave()` puts all three back. Up there the engine's own cloud deck (a `Clouds` object, `Cover` 0.62, `Density` 0.8), a low sun (`ClockTime` 17.5) and an `Atmosphere` of `Density` 0.3 give cloud shapes with a lit and a shaded side, a horizon and a sun to frame against. Presets: `void` (the black start), `sunset`, `gold` (the peak), `day`, `night`. `sky:to(name, seconds)` crosses between two, `sky:wind({ dir, speed })` streams thin wind streaks past in a travel shot (soft cloud puffs there read as blobs on a capture), `sky:leave()` puts the game's Lighting, Atmosphere, Sky, Clouds and post effects back. Measured: `Density` 0.42 with `Haze` 2.2 and a warm colour turned the same view into flat pink with teal patches, the look both judges marked down. `sky:sea()` adds a cloud sea under the stage (the engine has none): about 35 towers with lilac feet and warm lit heads in three bands of distance over a floor in the shadow tone, about 1,600 particles. The shadow tone is `shade` (default the preset's `Atmosphere.Decay`). Blind on one builder's cut recorded three ways (two judges, out of 50): this palette 37 and 32, all-warm tiers 35 and 33, the grey-lilac of pack 1.77 36 and 31; the violet floor swallowed a violet burst and the warm one the gold title rays, so pick `shade` off the hue of the cutscene's hero effect. `style = "clear"` keeps only the top two tiers over open air: it lost to the default in both of its sittings (64 against 71 and 68 against 72 of 100). The sea comes with a far bank of soft cloud 440-500 studs out round the stage that rises to about 10 degrees over it (1,160 particles more; `horizon = false` leaves it out). The bank covers the stepped slabs the engine's cloud deck draws near the horizon on low graphics (3k). Each layer is renewed by `Emit` every 18 s, so the sea holds for as long as the sky is entered (`density = 0.5` halves the counts; the frame cost on a phone is not measured). Call it once after `Sky.enter`; it follows the preset and stays hidden under `void`. **Lights on the stage**: after the jump up there, PointLights light nothing for 6.5-10 s (measured, see the rule under this table), so while a sky is entered every `fx_recipe` light is added to the ambient light for its frames instead (the character takes the effect's colour from the first frame, at any camera distance); `Sky.enter(preset, { lights = "local" })` keeps them as PointLights. `local face = sky:portrait(character)` puts a warm key and two coloured rims on the head for a close-up in the dark (`face.stop(0.3)`; options `key`, `rim`, `rim2`, `strength`). `sky:drift()` floats clusters of crystals round the stage at three depths, so a shot has something between the lens and the horizon (`{ along = dir, length = 200 }` for a flight; `count`, `size`, `color`) |
| Poses | `pose_beats.luau` | `PoseBeats.prepare(character)` makes a live avatar posable (a Motor6D twin for every AnimationConstraint joint, the Animate script and playing tracks stopped) and returns the function that undoes it. `PoseBeats.POSES` holds twelve general poses, each read on a front and a side capture: `stand`, `gaze` (still, chin up, looking at something: the quiet start and any face shot; `coil` drops the chin and hides the face), `rise`, `fly`, `bank` (a banked turn in flight; `PoseBeats.mirror(pose)` gives the other side), `coil`, `burst`, `hover`, `reach`, `hero`, `cheer`, `fall`. Key them (`{ t = 5.7, pose = "burst", ease = "outExpo" }` with `lib = PoseBeats.POSES`) and pass `sway = 2.5` so a held pose keeps breathing (in flight the legs kick and the arms trim on top of it) and `follow = 1` so the limbs arrive after the chest and settle: each joint follows its key on a spring, looser down the limbs (a 90 degree move eased out over 0.4 s reaches half-way 0.05 s late at the shoulder and 0.08 s at the wrist, and the wrist overshoots 8%; computed in Studio). `fly` and `bank` keep one arm ahead and lay the other back along the body (a flight with both arms out like wings was "stiff default flying poses" to the judges of the twelfth build) One `play` with every key of the scene, or `keep = true` on each: between two plays without it the rig stood in its rest pose (the default pose two judges saw in two shots), because a live avatar clears a written joint every frame. Anchor the root and move it yourself |
| A face | `pose_beats.luau` | `local face = PoseBeats.face(character)` then `face.set("joy", 0.2)` at the peak and `face.stop()` on the way out. `PoseBeats.FACES`: `neutral`, `smile`, `joy` (open smile), `awe` (round mouth, eyes wide), `grim`, `shut`. It writes `FaceControls` weights every frame (one write is gone on the next frame); read on close captures of one blocky head. A classic head with a face decal has no `FaceControls`: `face.has` is false and nothing changes |
| A counter and a title | `screen_overlays.luau` | `ScreenOverlays.counter({ to, land })` climbs in log space, ticks only its last digits at the end, never passes its last value, and lands at `land` with a punch and its last colour. Its last 0.9 s (`stretch`; 0 for one smooth climb) holds five values that close in on the last one (`steps`), each about 0.17 s on screen, and it lands white for two frames with a 1.2x punch (`punch`) and two four-point glints at opposite corners (`flare = false`: none); read on a 12 fps recording in Studio Play, 2026-11-22. `ScreenOverlays.title(name, { sub })` lands at 1.3 -> 1 and fades before its `dur` ends. Start the title at the counter's `land` or within 0.3 s of it |
| A way out | `screen_overlays.luau` | `ScreenOverlays.fade({ onCovered })` covers the screen over 0.5 s with the letterbox, the title and the counter kept on under the rising black, calls `onCovered` (undo the pose, `sky:leave()`, give the camera back) and fades the game in over 0.45 s. Give the title and the counter a `dur` that ends when the black is full (the fade's start + 0.5 s); it warns when either is already fading as it starts |
| A flash with a shape | `fx_recipe.luau` | The flash of `star_glint`, `shard_burst` and `reveal_star` is a star sprite, not a soft ball: caught on a still frame at `scale` 1.8 the old one was a white sphere 6 studs wide |
| A peak of its own | `cine_sky.luau`, `fx_recipe.luau` | 1.2-1.5 s in a speed tunnel with a ring of nine gold stars round the hero, seen from above: `sky:tunnel` and `FxRecipe.orbit(character, { recipe = "emblem" })`; comets round the hero: `FxRecipe.orbit(character)`. The shot and the code: "A peak of its own" at the end of this part |

From the builder's report: "a face 2 studs from the camera stayed dark brown with every point-light setting", and light levels judged in slow motion were wrong at full speed. The cause, measured afterwards (2026-11-21, a blank place, Studio Play, brightness read per frame from recordings):

| What moved (camera, Focus, subject and lights together) | PointLights dark for |
|---|---|
| 40 or 80 studs up; 350 or 450 studs sideways | no gap |
| 200 studs up | 6.5 s |
| 300 studs up | 8.5 s |
| 900 studs sideways | 7.5 s |
| 1,200 studs up in 15 steps of 80 over 1.5 s | 10 s |

Then every light comes on in one frame. Sun, ambient light and post effects are right from the first frame. One exception: with the camera within 8 studs of the subject and `Camera.Focus` within 3 studs of it, the lights drew at once at about two thirds of their later strength (gone by 16 studs of camera distance, and gone with Focus 5 studs off the subject). A slow-motion check gives the lights real seconds to arrive, which is why it misjudged them. Rules:

- A stage far from the play area (the sky stage, a boss arena reached by teleport) has no effect lights for the length of a short cutscene. On the sky stage this is handled: recipe lights become ambient pulses (`cine_sky`, table above). On a stage of your own, keep it within 80 studs of height and 450 of distance of where the camera was, move there 10 s early, or light the subject with ambient light and the sun.
- Judge light levels at 1x from a fresh start of the cutscene, before the frame-by-frame pass.
- A face in the dark: `gaze` pose, the camera 5-8 studs away, `sky:portrait(character)`; stop it when the shot ends. About 8 s after the entry its lights gain about half again, so it is for the opening shots.
- Recipe lights are set for a body seen from 10 studs and more: next to a face in a close-up play the recipe with `{ light = 0.4 }` (a share of every light layer; 0 for none).
- A star is never bigger than the frame it is seen in. `FxRecipe.play` fits the stars of a recipe to the camera every frame: the `spikes` that do not fly, the rings that do not lie on the floor, a crown's gap, the bursts and the z of each. `opts.fit` (default 2.6) is the longest arm as a share of half the frame's height at the anchor's depth, so the long arms of a star behind a head leave a 16:9 frame at its sides and the shorter ones show their points; a crown is held to half that, so its ring stays in the frame. Within 6 studs of the lens the share falls (to an eighth at 2 studs): nothing that near is large. `fit = false` on the play or on one layer turns it off. The same recipe and scale then hold in a wide shot and in a close-up (Studio Play stills, 2026-11-21: a `reveal_star` at scale 2.2 behind a head 5 studs from the camera drew the wide roots of 31-stud arms over the whole frame without it, and a whole star with its points with it). A recipe can carry its own share (`fit = 0.6` on `trail_star`: its head is a sparkle, not a burst).
- The rest of a recipe is held to the frame too. Trails (`tail` layers): the widest tail of a recipe is at most 8% of the frame height at its own depth and the others shrink with it, so a glow stays wider than its core; a tail goes out where any part of it passes within 4 studs of the lens. Within 9 studs of the lens a burst's or a stream's pieces are at most 1.2 of the frame's half height and a burst's fly at most half way to the lens (further out every recipe keeps its tuned sizes and throws). A gather sphere ends 0.3 of the anchor's distance short of the lens, its pieces slowed so they still meet at the anchor.
- In a cutscene, bands and stars in the nearer part of the gap between the lens and what the shot frames close: they fade from a fifth of the gap and are gone at 0.45 of it, anywhere in the frame (`FxClock.frontAt`; `cine_camera` sets the framed subject every frame, with `shot.clear = false` too). An orbit round a body therefore shows its sides and its far half; its near half passes the lens unseen in a close shot and is untouched from about 12 studs.
- Without an `opts.depth`, an effect whose anchor the camera sees in front of a kept-clear head (the head of a character `cine_camera` frames, or a part given to `FxRecipe.keepClear`) is drawn behind that head, where it shows round it as a rim. Pass `depth = 0` for one that must stay in front.
- `opts.owner = clock` (the cutscene's own clock, or the one `CineCamera.play` returned) stops the effect when that clock stops, so a crown started near the end does not stand over the game after the fade.
